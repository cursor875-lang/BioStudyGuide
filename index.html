📚 Core Concepts

1. Characteristics of Life

  • Order
  • Reproduction
  • Growth and development
  • Energy processing
  • Regulation
  • Response to the environment
  • Evolutionary adaptation

Remember: the cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.

2. Levels of Organization

Biosphere → Ecosystem → Community → Population → Organism → Organ System → Organ → Tissue → Cell → Organelle → Molecule

Population: same species in an area.
Community: different populations in an area.
Ecosystem: community + nonliving environmental factors.

3. Emergent Properties

A new property results from the arrangement and interactions of components.

Ask: What can the complete system do that its individual components cannot do independently?

4. Scientific Inquiry

TermMeaning
ObservationSomething detected or measured.
QuestionWhat the scientist wants to investigate.
HypothesisA proposed explanation for observations.
PredictionExpected result if the hypothesis is correct.
ExperimentA scientific test of a hypothesis.

5. Experimental Variables

ComponentAsk yourself
Independent variableWhat did the scientist change?
Dependent variableWhat did the scientist measure?
Controlled variablesWhat was kept similar?
Control groupWhat provides the comparison?
Experimental groupWho receives the treatment?

6. Qualitative vs. Quantitative

Qualitative: descriptive, nonnumerical — “the leaves are dark green.”

Quantitative: numerical measurement — “the plant grew 4.8 cm in seven days.”

7. Interpreting Results

  • Don't change results because they fail to support a hypothesis.
  • Reconsider or revise the hypothesis/design and collect more evidence.
  • One experiment does not permanently prove a hypothesis.
  • Repeated testing and multiple independent lines of evidence can increase confidence.

8. Observational Studies

Some questions cannot be tested with a controlled experiment. Scientists can use observations such as DNA sequences, fossils, populations, and human health records.

9. Science Is Not a Straight Line

Scientific inquiry is repetitive, nonlinear, and collaborative. Scientists can move between exploration, hypotheses, testing, evidence analysis, feedback, and new questions.

10. Evolution & Natural Selection

Natural selection depends on heritable variation, overproduction of offspring, and unequal reproductive success.

Individuals do not evolve because they need to change. Populations evolve over generations.

11. Flow of Information

DNA stores hereditary information and provides instructions involved in protein production. Information can also move through organisms as signals and pathways.

  • DNA from parents to offspring
  • Genes influencing protein production
  • Hormonal signaling
  • Cellular communication
  • Environmental signals affecting cell activity

12. Structure & Function

Structure is related to function at all levels of biological organization.

Test move: If you see an unfamiliar structure, ask how its shape or organization helps it perform its function.

13. Energy & Matter

ENERGY FLOWS
Sunlight → Producers → Consumers → Heat

MATTER CYCLES
Environment → Producers → Consumers → Decomposers → Environment

Don't mix them up: energy generally moves one direction; matter is recycled.

14. Interactions

A change to one component can influence many others.

Drought → fewer plants → fewer insects → fewer frogs → fewer predators

15. Systems Biology

Studies how interacting components produce complex biological behavior. Scientists may combine observations, environmental measurements, mathematical models, computer models, and population data.
